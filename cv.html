cv

Statistician with experience building interpretable models for complex observational data, and the software to fit them at scale. I currently lead the modelling and engineering behind the Biodiversity Intactness Index (BII) in the Biodiversity Futures Lab at the Natural History Museum, London.

I have a PhD in statistics from the University of Western Australia and was a postdoc at the University of Cambridge, where I developed Bayesian methods for combining mechanistic models with data when those models are known to be wrong.

Statistical modelling

  • Hierarchical models for large observational datasets. Lead the mixed-effects modelling behind the BII: over 4 million observations from more than 35,000 sites across many independent studies, projected globally with uncertainty.
    predictsr · ssmooth
  • Inference under model misspecification. Developed the statistical finite element method, a Bayesian framework that embeds a Gaussian process discrepancy term in a mechanistic model so that inference stays honest when the model is wrong.
    PNAS, 2021 · paper
  • Scalable Bayesian computation. Built low-rank approximations and Langevin-based sampling schemes that make posterior inference tractable for high-dimensional models.
    J. Comput. Phys., 2022 · paper · SIAM/ASA JUQ, 2022 · paper
  • Learning from unstructured data. Co-developed physics-informed variational autoencoders that link raw, unstructured observations to interpretable latent dynamics.
    J. Comput. Phys., 2024 · paper

Experience

  • Senior Research Software Engineer — Natural History Museum, London (2026–present)
    • Lead the statistical modelling and technical development of the BII, a global biodiversity indicator built on mixed-effects models from over 4 million observations, from over 35 thousand sites from the PREDICTS database.
    • Own the model specification, validation, and uncertainty propagation from site-level data to global projections.
    • Maintain the BII’s R packages and HPC/AWS deployment, and work with ecologists to turn research questions into models.
  • Research Software Engineer — Natural History Museum, London (2024–2026)
    • Refactored the BII pipeline into tested R packages and deployed it on HPC.
    • Scaled projections from 10 km to 1 km resolution.
    • Published two CRAN packages (predictsr, ssmooth) and added CI, testing, documentation, and QA.
  • Postdoctoral Research Associate — University of Cambridge (2021–2024)
    • Recruited to Mark Girolami’s group in the final year of my PhD; completed the thesis alongside the first year of the postdoc.
    • Developed Bayesian inference methods (MCMC, Langevin dynamics, filtering) for complex physical models, with a focus on model misspecification and uncertainty quantification.
    • Published in PNAS, SIAM/ASA JUQ, and the Journal of Computational Physics.
  • Teaching Assistant — University of Western Australia (2018–2020)
    • Taught undergraduate and master’s statistics courses through tutorials, assessment setting, and exam marking.
